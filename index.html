How the numbers are made

Ratings become probabilities through P = rating^k / Σ rating^k — a Luce model, the same shape real power-rating books use.

k is the spread dial, and it works on ratios, not gaps. It never changes the order, only how far apart the prices sit. Rating your teams 88 and 30 is a 2.9× ratio; 88 and 80 is 1.1× and barely separates at all. So the scale you rate on matters as much as k. Pick a k that puts your best team somewhere you'd actually bet — +250 to +400 in a ten-team league — then leave it alone and move the ratings, not the model.

Last place is not "doesn't win." Inverting 1−p would be wrong. The same model runs with the exponent's sign flipped, rating^−k, so the worst team becomes the favourite and the two boards stay internally consistent. That inversion compresses the good teams and blows out the bad one, which is why the last-place pool gets its own k — usually a lower one.

Vig is a multiplier on every probability before it becomes a price. At 8% the implied percentages sum to 108% and that 8% is your hold. 4.76% is the standard ten-cent line: −110 implies 110/210 = 52.38%, and 0.5238/0.50 = 1.0476. For a friendly league 0% usually plays better. The slider steps by 0.25 and the box takes any value, so exact holds are reachable.

The cap floors a price, which costs you hold. +2500 is a 3.85% implied floor; anything longer gets lifted to it, and the book total above counts what that actually costs. Longshots past about +5000 look silly and nobody bets them.

Tidy rounds to the increments a real board quotes — nearest 5 up to 400, then 10, then 25. The probabilities shown are read back off the posted price, so every column agrees with what you'd actually be laying.

Picking ratings

Start from points scored, not record. Fantasy record is mostly schedule luck — a 4–4 team that has scored the most points in the league is better than a 6–2 team sitting eighth. Points per game is the most predictive single number you have.

The rescaling matters more than the source. Raw PPG is compressed: 130 against 100 is only 1.3×, which barely separates at any sane k. From points mode converts to z-scores and re-centres — 50 + 15 × (PPG − league avg) / stdev — mapping a normal league onto roughly 20–80. A league where everyone scores alike produces a naturally flat board, which is correct.

Shrink early. Three games of scoring is mostly noise. Fill the Preseason column and the mode blends toward it by w = games / (games + 4) — at week 3 the prior still carries 57%, and by week 8 it has washed out. Leave the column empty and no shrink is applied.

Then adjust by hand, sparingly. A season-ending injury or a single 190-point week deserves a nudge. Cap yourself at about ±8 points so the model still does the work. If your eye and the model disagree on third, trust the model; if they disagree on first, go find the outlier week driving it.

Decide up front whether the ratings are published. If the league can see the inputs they'll argue about the ratings instead of the odds, which is the healthier fight. Copy link packs the whole board into a URL — nothing is uploaded, and anyone who opens it sees exactly your inputs.
